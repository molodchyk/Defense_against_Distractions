# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Zhang, J., & Norman, D. A. (1994). Representations in Distributed Cognitive Tasks. Cognitive Science, 18(1), 87-122.

Link: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog1801_3

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog1801_3

## Source Type

- theory / experimental cognitive science
- external representations

## Research Context

The paper studies tasks where cognition is distributed across internal mental representations and external representations in the environment.

## Main Finding

External representations can change the nature and difficulty of cognitive tasks by making information visible, persistent, and easier to manipulate.

## Limitations

The paper is not about web browsing, digital wellbeing, or self-control. The application to DaD is indirect.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

The intent graph turns an invisible browsing chain into an external representation. This can make drift inspectable and can reduce the user's need to reconstruct the path from memory.

## Product Implication

Show graph should be designed as a compact external representation:

- origin;
- current page;
- last coherent page;
- first drift point;
- reasons;
- transition structure;
- recovery actions.

It should not overload the user with raw history.

## Claim Boundary

What DaD may safely say:

Graph view helps make the local browsing trajectory visible for inspection.

What DaD must not claim:

DaD must not claim that graph view has been directly validated as a clinical or productivity intervention.

## Notes

This supports the design grammar of Show graph.
