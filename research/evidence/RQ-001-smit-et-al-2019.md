# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Smit, E. S., Zeidler, C., Resnicow, K., & de Vries, H. (2019). Identifying the Most Autonomy-Supportive Message Frame in Digital Health Communication: A 2x2 Between-Subjects Experiment. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 21(10), e14074.

Link: https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e14074/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2196/14074

## Source Type

- primary study
- digital communication experiment

## Research Context

The study tested autonomy-supportive language and provision of choice in a digital health communication setting.

## Main Finding

The study found that providing choice can be an easy-to-implement way to improve digital communication effectiveness, especially for people with a high need for autonomy.

## Empirical Detail

- Design: Web-based 2 x 2 between-subjects experiment.
- Sample: 525 Dutch adults in the final analyzed sample.
- Manipulations: autonomy-supportive versus controlling language, crossed with choice versus no choice.
- Outcomes: perceived autonomy support, perceived relevance, and overall intervention evaluation.
- Result: choice improved overall evaluation (b = .12, P = .003); for participants high in need for autonomy, choice improved perceived relevance (b = .13, P = .02). Language manipulation did not significantly affect the three outcomes.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The concrete choice mattered more than merely using autonomy-supportive language. For DaD, this suggests that "nice copy" cannot substitute for real bounded options during enforcement.

## Limitations

This is a health-message framing study, not a locked-schedule study. It does not imply that DaD should always offer an immediate unrestricted choice during a lock.

## Evidence Grade

weak to moderate for DaD.

## Relevance To DaD

Even when relaxation is blocked during a locked schedule, DaD can preserve autonomy by offering meaningful choices that do not weaken protection:

- add protection;
- keep current settings;
- wait until lock ends;
- open diagnostics;
- use emergency escape if configured;
- review after the schedule.

## Design Consequence

DaD's locked-state UI should avoid a dead end. If the user cannot relax the plan now, the interface should still show useful, safe actions.

## What Changes

Locked schedule enforcement should preserve a bounded action space: strengthen protection, inspect why the lock is active, queue a change, wait until the lock ends, or use an explicit emergency path. This changes the UI requirement from "deny relaxation" to "deny relaxation while preserving agency-compatible options."

## Notes

This is a practical UI source: if there is enforcement, keep some action space.
