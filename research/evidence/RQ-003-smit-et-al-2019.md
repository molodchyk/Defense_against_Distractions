# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Smit, E. S., Zeidler, C., Resnicow, K., & de Vries, H. (2019). Identifying the Most Autonomy-Supportive Message Frame in Digital Health Communication: A 2x2 Between-Subjects Experiment. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 21(10), e14074.

Link: https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e14074/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2196/14074

## Source Type

- primary experiment
- digital health communication

## Research Context

The study tested two autonomy-support strategies in a Web-based health intervention: autonomy-supportive versus controlling language, crossed with choice versus no choice.

## Main Finding

Provision of choice improved overall intervention evaluation; autonomy-supportive language did not significantly affect any of the three measured outcomes.

## Empirical Detail

- Design: Web-based 2x2 between-subjects experiment.
- Final sample: 525 Dutch adults.
- Outcomes: perceived autonomy support, perceived relevance, overall intervention evaluation.
- Choice improved overall evaluation (b=.12, P=.003).
- For high-need-autonomy participants, choice improved perceived relevance (b=.13, P=.02).
- Choice's effect on perceived autonomy support approached significance (b=.07, P=.07).
- Language manipulation had no significant effects on the three outcomes.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The action architecture mattered more than the tone. "Autonomy-supportive language" did not substitute for a real choice in this digital setting.

## Limitations

This was health communication, not enforcement. It does not imply that DaD should offer the forbidden action during a locked schedule.

## Evidence Grade

moderate for DaD UI/action design.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not solve autonomy by writing nicer copy while leaving the user at a dead end.

## Design Consequence

During enforcement, offer real bounded actions that preserve the active commitment:

- Return;
- Show graph;
- queue relaxation;
- add stricter protection;
- wait until unlock;
- emergency escape if configured.

## What Changes

The UI standard becomes "bounded agency," not "softened wording."
