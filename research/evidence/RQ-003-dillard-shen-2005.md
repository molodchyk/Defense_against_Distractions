# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Dillard, J. P., & Shen, L. (2005). On the Nature of Reactance and its Role in Persuasive Health Communication. Communication Monographs, 72(2), 144-168.

Link: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03637750500111815

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/03637750500111815

## Source Type

- primary studies
- communication psychology

## Research Context

The paper examines psychological reactance in health communication, including anger and negative cognitions as components of reactance.

## Main Finding

Reactance can be understood through both anger and negative cognitions. Freedom-threatening communication can undermine persuasive messages by increasing resistance.

## Limitations

The paper focuses on messages, not software enforcement. DaD's strongest restrictions are not persuasion alone; they are precommitted controls.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD's copy can either reduce or amplify reactance. Language like "you are trying to bypass" or "you cannot be trusted" can trigger anger and counterarguing even if the enforcement rule itself is reasonable.

## Product Implication

DaD should avoid accusatory copy. Enforcement text should describe the rule, not judge the person:

- "This locked schedule is active."
- "This change would weaken the active plan."
- "Relaxation is available after the lock ends."
