# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Adamczyk, P. D., & Bailey, B. P. (2004). If Not Now, When? The Effects of Interruption at Different Moments Within Task Execution. Proceedings of CHI 2004, 271-278.

Link: https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Adamczyk-CHI04-p271-adamczyk.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985727

## Source Type

- primary study
- HCI interruption research

## Research Context

The study interrupted users at different predicted moments during task execution to measure performance, emotional state, workload, and social attribution.

## Main Finding

Interruption timing matters. Interruptions at predicted better moments produced less annoyance, frustration, time pressure, mental effort, and disruption than interruptions at worse moments.

## Empirical Detail

- Tasks included structured activities such as web searching, document editing, and media tasks.
- Task models were used to identify predicted interruption moments.
- Measures included task performance, emotional state, workload, and attribution.
- Result direction: predicted best interruption moments consistently caused less disruption and negative affect than poor/random moments.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The same intervention can be harmful or tolerable depending on where it lands in the task structure. Timing is part of intervention quality, not just a delivery detail.

## Limitations

Laboratory tasks are simpler than real browsing. The paper does not directly define browser intent drift.

## Evidence Grade

moderate to strong.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD prompts are themselves interruptions. Even a correct prompt can be costly if injected at the wrong moment.

## Design Consequence

Prompt timing should be modeled: prefer navigation boundaries, new-tab creation, page-load completion, idle-after-load, feed-scroll thresholds, or media-start boundaries. Avoid non-hard prompts during active typing when feasible.

## What Changes

This adds prompt timing as a scoring/policy input. It is not enough to know whether to prompt; DaD should also know when to prompt.

## Notes

This source should influence on-page prompt scheduling and intervention severity.
