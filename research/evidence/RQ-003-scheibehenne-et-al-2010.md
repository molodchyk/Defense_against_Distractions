# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Scheibehenne, B., Greifeneder, R., & Todd, P. M. (2010). Can There Ever Be Too Many Options? A Meta-Analytic Review of Choice Overload. Journal of Consumer Research, 37(3), 409-425.

Link: https://scheibehenne.com/ScheibehenneGreifenederTodd2010.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/651235

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- choice overload research

## Research Context

The paper synthesized published and unpublished experiments on whether larger choice sets reduce motivation, choice satisfaction, or choice commitment.

## Main Finding

The mean effect size of choice overload was virtually zero, with considerable variance between studies. Potential preconditions were identified, but no sufficient conditions.

## Empirical Detail

- Scope: 63 conditions from 50 experiments.
- Sample: N=5,036.
- Result: mean effect size virtually zero.
- Heterogeneity: substantial variance between studies.
- Candidate moderators included preference clarity, dominant options, familiarity, and choice-set structure.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The practical question is not "more choice versus less choice." It is whether the user has clear preferences, enough structure, and a dominant safe action.

## Limitations

This is consumer-choice research, not browser lockout research. It does not specify optimal button counts.

## Evidence Grade

strong for correcting overgeneralized choice-overload claims; moderate for DaD design.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not blindly minimize choice. It should provide enough action to preserve agency, but structure the moment so the next good action is obvious.

## Design Consequence

Use small visible action sets for high-pressure enforcement surfaces, but put real secondary options behind inspect/review flows.

## What Changes

The product rule becomes "bounded, structured choice," not "few choices at all costs."

## Notes

This card keeps the research from turning into a simplistic UI doctrine.
