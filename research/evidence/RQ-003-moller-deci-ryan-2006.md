# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Moller, A. C., Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2006). Choice and Ego-Depletion: The Moderating Role of Autonomy. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 32(8), 1024-1036.

Link: https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/2006_MollerDeciRyan_ChoiceandEgo-Depletion.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167206288008

## Source Type

- primary experimental studies
- self-determination theory / self-control

## Research Context

The paper tested whether choice is depleting in the same way when choice feels autonomous versus controlled.

## Main Finding

Controlled-choice conditions were ego-depleting, while autonomous-choice conditions did not show the same depletion pattern. In one experiment, perceived self-determination mediated the relation between choice condition and depletion.

## Empirical Detail

- Studies: three experiments.
- Theory contrast: self-regulatory strength model versus self-determination theory.
- Key distinction: autonomous choice versus controlled choice.
- Experiment 3 found mediation by perceived self-determination between choice condition and ego-depletion as measured by performance.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Choice itself is not automatically autonomy-supportive. A choice can become a self-control cost when the person experiences it as pressured or controlled.

## Limitations

The ego-depletion literature has replication debates, and these experiments do not directly test software prompts. The mechanism is still useful for prompt design.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not flood enforcement screens with many decisions and call that autonomy.

## Design Consequence

Prompts should present a small set of decisive actions:

- recover;
- branch;
- inspect;
- defer/queue;
- emergency escape only where appropriate.

## What Changes

DaD should optimize for controlled choice reduction: fewer, clearer, policy-valid actions under pressure.

## Notes

This source prevents an overcorrection from "hard lock" to "too many choices."
