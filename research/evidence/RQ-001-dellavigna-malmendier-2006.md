# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: DellaVigna, S., & Malmendier, U. (2006). Paying Not to Go to the Gym. American Economic Review, 96(3), 694-719.

Link: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.96.3.694

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.3.694

## Source Type

- primary study
- behavioral economics
- contract-choice analysis

## Research Context

The authors analyzed contract choice and actual attendance for health-club members over three years.

## Main Finding

Many consumers chose gym contracts inconsistent with their later behavior. Flat-fee monthly members attended too little to justify the contract relative to cheaper pay-per-visit options and delayed cancellation despite paying for flexibility.

## Empirical Detail

- Dataset: 7,752 members at three U.S. health clubs.
- Time horizon: three years of contract and attendance data.
- Monthly flat-fee users: attended 4.3 times per month on average.
- Cost mismatch: paid more than 17 dollars per expected visit despite a 10-dollar visit pass.
- Forgone savings: about 600 dollars during membership.
- Retention surprise: monthly members were 17 percent more likely to stay beyond one year than annual members, despite paying for the monthly cancellation option.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

People may buy flexibility while failing to use it, or buy commitment-like contracts while overestimating future use. Contract choice can reflect future-use optimism more than stable preference.

## Limitations

Gym contracts are sold by firms and are not pure self-control tools. The study is observational, not a randomized lock intervention.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD users may overestimate their future ability to follow a plan, cancel a lock, review a schedule, or use a break correctly. Future self-prediction is unreliable even with money involved.

## Design Consequence

Before changing lock defaults, DaD should show local historical outcomes: conflict counts, emergency escapes, immediate post-lock softening, and repeated relaxation attempts.

## What Changes

This supports "past behavior feedback" in schedule setup. DaD should not rely only on the user's current prediction about future focus.

## Notes

This is a useful source for why configuration UX should surface history, not just intentions.
