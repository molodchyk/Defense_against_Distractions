# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Reynolds-Tylus, T. (2019). Psychological Reactance and Persuasive Health Communication: A Review of the Literature. Frontiers in Communication, 4, 56.

Link: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00056/full

DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomm.2019.00056

## Source Type

- review
- communication psychology

## Research Context

The review summarizes reactance theory and its application to health communication. It discusses freedom threat, message features, anger, counterarguing, and restoration of threatened freedom.

## Main Finding

Messages and interventions that threaten freedom can provoke reactance. Reactance can reduce acceptance and encourage people to restore restricted behavior.

## Limitations

The review focuses on communication rather than browser extension enforcement. DaD also differs because some restrictions are precommitted by the user.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not rely on "because I said so" UI. If the tool blocks, delays, or hides something, it needs to show enough context for the user to understand the action.

## Product Implication

DaD's enforcement surfaces should include:

- reason;
- target;
- active plan or schedule;
- safe action;
- no moral accusation;
- no hidden state.
