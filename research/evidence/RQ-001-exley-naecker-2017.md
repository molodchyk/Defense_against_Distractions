# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Exley, C. L., & Naecker, J. K. (2017). Observability Increases the Demand for Commitment Devices. Management Science, 63(10), 3262-3267.

Link: https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2501

PDF: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/ExleyNaecker_Observability_20f397e8-4872-4d39-b957-75cd5e77a205.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2016.2501

## Source Type

- primary study
- field experiment
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

Participants signing up for a workshop could choose how much money to forgo if they did not attend. In the private condition, only the participant knew the commitment level. In the public condition, the person running the workshop also knew.

## Main Finding

Public observability increased demand for commitment. Commitment choices were partly shaped by signaling, not only by private self-control sophistication.

## Empirical Detail

- Private condition: 41 percent demanded some commitment.
- Public condition: 65 percent demanded some commitment.
- Average commitment level: 5.17 dollars in private vs 8.87 dollars in public.
- The action target was workshop attendance; the commitment choice was a forfeitable amount from 0 to 15 dollars.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

A commitment request can be reputational theater. The user may choose a stricter contract because it is visible and identity-relevant, not because it fits their future failure mode.

## Limitations

Workshop attendance and public commitment are not the same as private browser protection. But the mechanism matters if DaD later supports shared configs, public templates, testimonials, leaderboards, or strictness badges.

## Evidence Grade

moderate to strong.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should avoid social features that reward visible strictness. Public strictness can distort calibration.

## Design Consequence

Keep lock outcomes and strictness private by default. If DaD supports config sharing, share purpose and context, not "hardcore" status.

## What Changes

This changes community-feature design: ranking configs by strictness or showing public streak/failure metrics is not neutral; it may increase performative overcommitment.

## Notes

This is one of the clearest sources against social strictness signaling.
