# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Monsell, S. (2003). Task switching. Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 7(3), 134-140.

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12639695/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S1364-6613(03)00028-7

## Source Type

- review
- cognitive psychology

## Research Context

The review summarizes research on the control processes involved in switching between tasks, including task-set reconfiguration and switch costs.

## Main Finding

Switching tasks produces measurable costs, even when people know a switch is coming. Cognitive control has to reconfigure task sets, and residual activation from the previous task can interfere.

## Limitations

Many task-switching studies use simple laboratory tasks. The effect does not map one-to-one onto complex web browsing, and switching can sometimes be useful or necessary.

## Evidence Grade

strong for task-switching costs; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should treat rapid tab switching, A/B loops, repeated navigation loops, and abrupt chain shifts as plausible fragmentation signals. These should be bounded contributors, not proof of failure.

## Product Implication

Task-switching signals should remain modifiers unless they combine with other drift evidence such as low origin similarity, passive media pressure, recommender clicks, or stale control.

## Claim Boundary

What DaD may safely say:

Frequent switching can carry cognitive costs, so DaD uses bounded switching and loop signals as part of local drift diagnostics.

What DaD must not claim:

DaD must not claim all switching is bad or that task-switch costs prove a user is distracted.

## Notes

This source supports signals, not hard blocking by itself.
