# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Teevan, J., Alvarado, C., Ackerman, M. S., & Karger, D. R. (2004). The Perfect Search Engine Is Not Enough: A Study of Orienteering Behavior in Directed Search. Proceedings of CHI 2004, 415-422.

Link: https://people.csail.mit.edu/teevan/work/publications/papers/chi04.pdf

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/985692.985745

## Source Type

- primary study
- HCI
- information seeking

## Research Context

The authors studied how expert users searched their own personal information spaces and the web through repeated interviews and observation.

## Main Finding

Even when people knew what they were looking for, they often did not jump directly to it. They used orienteering: small situated steps through remembered sources, context, and landmarks.

## Empirical Detail

- Sample: 15 MIT computer science graduate students.
- Data: 1512 semi-structured interviews over five weekdays, plus observation and longer interviews.
- Keyword search appeared in only 39 percent of searches.
- Participants often searched for information sources, not the exact target, because they remembered more about the source than the target.
- Orienteering allowed users to specify less of the information need up front and use contextual information.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Low semantic similarity can be part of a rational path. A user may navigate away from the apparent target because the route to the answer goes through a contextual source or landmark.

## Limitations

The sample was expert and small. The study concerns directed search, not distraction defense.

## Evidence Grade

strong for orienteering as a real search behavior; strong for DaD false-positive design.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD must distinguish drift from legitimate branch exploration. Isolate and Mark coherent are not mere leniency; they are necessary because many useful web paths are indirect.

## Design Consequence

Low similarity alone should not trigger hard intervention. The system needs branch recovery actions and user-confirmed new baselines.

## What Changes

This strengthens Isolate as a core action and argues for treating search-query/source context as a modifier.

## Notes

Use this source whenever product discussions collapse exploration into distraction.
