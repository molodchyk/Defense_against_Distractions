# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Rosenberg, B. D., & Siegel, J. T. (2018). A 50-Year Review of Psychological Reactance Theory: Do Not Read This Article. Motivation Science, 4(4), 281-300.

Link: https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2017-43845-001

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/mot0000091

## Source Type

- review
- psychological theory

## Research Context

The paper reviews fifty years of psychological reactance theory. Reactance occurs when people perceive a threat to freedom and become motivated to restore that freedom.

## Main Finding

Reactance is a robust framework for understanding resistance to perceived control. It can appear as anger, counterarguing, derogating the source, or behavior aimed at restoring a threatened option.

## Limitations

The review is broad. It does not directly test browser blockers, locked schedules, or DaD's specific interfaces.

## Evidence Grade

strong for the theory; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD's strongest interventions are freedom restrictions. If they feel externally imposed or contemptuous, users may fight the tool rather than experience it as protection.

## Product Implication

DaD should treat unexplained or moralizing enforcement as a product risk. Strong enforcement needs:

- prior user choice;
- visible reason;
- visible scope;
- visible end condition;
- safe remaining actions.
