# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Parry, D. A., & le Roux, D. B. (2021). "Cognitive Control in Media Multitaskers" Ten Years On: A Meta-Analysis. Cyberpsychology: Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace, 15(2), Article 7.

Link: https://cyberpsychology.eu/article/view/13303

PDF: https://cyberpsychology.eu/article/download/13303/32657/30754

DOI: https://doi.org/10.5817/CP2021-2-7

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- psychology
- media multitasking

## Research Context

The meta-analysis re-evaluated the decade of work after Ophir, Nass, and Wagner's 2009 media multitasking study, focusing on media multitasking and cognitive control.

## Main Finding

The evidence is mixed and modest. Associations with some cognitive-control outcomes exist, but they are small and heterogeneous. The authors report no causal conclusion, and no associations for interference management or task management in their summary.

## Empirical Detail

- Earlier performance-based meta-analysis summarized in the paper found a small association between media multitasking and distractibility (`d = 0.17`).
- The paper reports small associations in some categories and stronger effects for self-report than performance-based measures.
- Summary finding: no associations for interference management or task management; small associations for sustained attention and working memory.
- The paper states that available designs do not establish causal direction or mechanism.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The popular narrative is stronger than the evidence. Media multitasking may correlate with some cognitive-control problems, but the signal is too weak and heterogeneous to classify a browsing moment by itself.

## Limitations

The meta-analysis aggregates varied measures and media-use definitions. It is not a browser-session intervention study.

## Evidence Grade

strong for limiting claims; moderate for media-pressure scoring.

## Relevance To DaD

Media/feed pressure should remain a modifier, not a primary detector or hard-block trigger.

## Design Consequence

Require convergence with trajectory evidence: origin decay, drift depth, descendant tabs, passive interaction, or locked policy.
