# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Derksen, L., Kerwin, J. T., Ordaz Reynoso, N., & Sterck, O. (2025). Healthcare Appointments as Commitment Devices. The Economic Journal, 135(665), 81-118.

Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/econjl/v135y2025i665p81-118..html

Working paper PDF: https://docs.iza.org/dp17070.pdf

## Source Type

- primary study
- randomized field experiment
- health behavior
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

High-risk men in Malawi were randomly offered HIV-testing appointments, hard commitment devices, or both. The study compares ordinary appointments with financial commitment.

## Main Finding

Appointments can act as effective substitutes for hard commitment devices. Appointments more than doubled HIV testing rates, with effects concentrated among those who demanded commitment. Most men taking hard commitments lost their investments.

## Empirical Detail

- Setting: HIV testing among high-risk men in Malawi.
- Design: random assignment to control, appointments, hard commitment, or combined treatment.
- Published abstract result: appointments more than doubled testing rates.
- Working-paper detail: participants numbered 1,232 in a later public draft.
- Key contrast: hard commitment increased testing less than appointment-style structure, and most hard-commitment adopters lost their deposits.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The softer device can outperform the harder one because appointments reduce planning, memory, and initiation friction while avoiding penalty failure. Stronger punishment is not automatically stronger protection.

## Limitations

This is health-care behavior, not browser use. The social and logistical meaning of an appointment may not map perfectly to software.

## Evidence Grade

strong for soft-vs-hard commitment comparison; moderate for DaD.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not make hard lockout the only serious form of defense. Scheduled review, return-at-time, queued change review, and appointment-like anchors may be real commitment devices.

## Design Consequence

Add a ladder of commitment modes: soft scheduled structure, delayed review, queued relaxation, hard locked schedule, and emergency escape. Do not treat hard lock as the only mature feature.

## What Changes

This changes the roadmap: post-lock review and scheduled return are not UX polish; they are mechanism-bearing commitment infrastructure.

## Notes

This source is unusually important because it directly compares hard and soft commitment.
