# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Ariely, D., & Wertenbroch, K. (2002). Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance: Self-Control by Precommitment. Psychological Science, 13(3), 219-224.

Link: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9280.00441

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00441

## Source Type

- primary study
- experiments
- psychology
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

The paper studies whether people self-impose costly deadlines, whether those deadlines reduce procrastination, and whether self-imposed deadlines are as effective as externally structured deadlines.

## Main Finding

People use precommitment against procrastination, and it can improve performance. But self-selected deadlines are often not optimal; evenly spaced external deadlines performed better in the studied task.

## Empirical Detail

- Study domain: deadline choices for academic-style assignments and proof-reading tasks.
- Intervention: participants could choose deadlines that carried performance penalties if missed.
- Direction of effect: self-imposed deadlines improved outcomes relative to no deadlines, but externally imposed evenly spaced deadlines performed better.
- Mechanism measured indirectly: deadline selection and later task performance under penalties.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Users may correctly detect that they need structure while still choosing poor structure. The failure mode is not absence of motivation; it is calibration of timing and constraint.

## Limitations

The tasks are not browser usage, and later replication work creates some uncertainty around exact effect size. The robust design lesson is narrower: self-authored structure can help, but UI should not make the user invent every detail from scratch.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

Locked schedules are deadline-like commitments. They must be authored by the user, but DaD should help with timing and strength rather than assuming the user's first schedule is well-calibrated.

## Design Consequence

DaD should provide lock templates, previews, and post-lock review. "User configured it" is not enough; the product should help the user choose a workable schedule.

## What Changes

This weakens any design assumption that DaD can simply expose raw lock controls and trust users to tune them perfectly. It strengthens the case for schedule templates and calibration feedback.

## Notes

Use this as a calibration source, not as a blanket argument for external coercion.
