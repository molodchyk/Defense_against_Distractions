# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Ariely, D., & Wertenbroch, K. (2002). Procrastination, Deadlines, and Performance: Self-Control by Precommitment. Psychological Science, 13(3), 219-224.

Link: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/1467-9280.00441

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.00441

## Source Type

- primary study
- experiments
- psychology / behavioral economics

## Research Context

The paper experimentally studies whether people self-impose costly deadlines, whether those deadlines improve performance, and whether people set them optimally.

## Main Finding

Participants were willing to impose costly deadlines on themselves, and those self-imposed deadlines helped reduce procrastination. However, participants did not set deadlines optimally, and externally imposed deadlines performed better in the studied tasks.

## Limitations

The studies focus on academic-style deadlines, not digital distraction or browser locks. Externally imposed deadlines being more effective does not automatically justify hostile enforcement in personal software; the source mainly shows that self-set commitment can help but may need structure.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

Locked schedules are similar to self-imposed deadlines. The finding that people want precommitment supports allowing users to create protected schedules. The finding that people do not set deadlines optimally supports providing sensible defaults, previews, warnings, and post-lock review.

## Product Implication

DaD should:

- keep user-created locked schedules;
- show a clear preview of what will be blocked during the lock;
- explain that relaxation will be delayed or blocked during the active period;
- avoid making locked setup too easy to misconfigure;
- provide a post-lock adjustment path.

The current "stricter edits allowed" rule is reasonable because it does not undermine the original deadline-like commitment.

## Claim Boundary

What DaD may safely say:

Precommitted deadlines and restrictions can help people reduce procrastination, but they need careful setup.

What DaD must not claim:

DaD must not claim that user-set locks will always be optimal or that stricter defaults are always better.

## Notes

This source is especially important for UI design: the setup screen should help the user choose the commitment while calm.
