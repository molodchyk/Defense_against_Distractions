# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Royer, H., Stehr, M., & Sydnor, J. (2015). Incentives, Commitments, and Habit Formation in Exercise: Evidence from a Field Experiment with Workers at a Fortune-500 Company. American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 7(3), 51-84.

Link: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.20130327

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/app.20130327

## Source Type

- primary study
- field experiment
- workplace health
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

Workers at a Fortune 500 company participated in a gym-use experiment testing incentives and self-funded commitment contracts.

## Main Finding

Short-run incentives alone had limited long-run persistence. Adding commitment after the incentive program generated detectable long-run changes, including effects several years later.

## Empirical Detail

- Setting: workplace gym use.
- Intervention: four-week incentive program, with some participants offered a commitment contract afterward.
- Result: incentives produced strong short-run effects but small lasting changes; the commitment-contract condition showed significant long-run changes.
- Time horizon: the abstract reports effects detectable several years after the incentive ended.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Motivation spikes and durable self-regulation are different. Commitment may preserve behavior after the initial stimulus fades, but only if it becomes part of repeated structure.

## Limitations

Gym attendance is not browser behavior. The paper does not define how often DaD should review locks or how long digital commitments should last.

## Evidence Grade

moderate to strong.

## Relevance To DaD

One intense focus session is not habit formation. DaD needs repeated schedule structure and review if it is meant to become a real exoskeleton rather than a one-off blocker.

## Design Consequence

Add recurring lock review and plan history. Treat repeated protected sessions as a learning process, not simply repeated enforcement.

## What Changes

This supports tracking plan-level outcomes over time and makes "review after repeated use" more important than a single perfect setup screen.

Relevant for future local validation metrics and plan dashboards.
