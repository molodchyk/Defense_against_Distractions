# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Ryan, R. M., & Deci, E. L. (2000). Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation, Social Development, and Well-Being. American Psychologist, 55(1), 68-78.

Link: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11392867/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68

## Source Type

- theory / review
- motivation psychology

## Research Context

Self-determination theory explains motivation in relation to autonomy, competence, and relatedness. For DaD, autonomy and competence are most relevant.

## Main Finding

People function better when behavior is experienced as self-endorsed rather than externally controlled. Structure can support autonomy when it connects to the person's own values, goals, and choices.

## Limitations

This is broad motivational theory. It does not test DaD, browser blocks, or locked schedules.

## Evidence Grade

moderate for DaD design.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD's strong protection can remain autonomy-supportive when it is clearly rooted in the user's own earlier plan. It becomes autonomy-threatening when rules feel arbitrary, hidden, permanent, or judgmental.

## Product Implication

DaD should communicate ownership:

- "Your locked schedule is active."
- "Your plan currently blocks this."
- "This would weaken the active plan."

It should preserve competence by making recovery options legible.

## Notes

This source defines the positive target: not merely less reactance, but more self-endorsed structure.
