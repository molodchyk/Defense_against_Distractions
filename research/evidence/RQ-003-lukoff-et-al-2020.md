# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Lukoff, K., Lyngs, U., Gueorguieva, S., Dillman, E. S., Hiniker, A., & Munson, S. A. (2020). From Ancient Contemplative Practice to the App Store: Designing a Digital Container for Mindfulness. Proceedings of the 2020 ACM Designing Interactive Systems Conference, 1551-1564.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09521

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3357236.3395444

## Source Type

- HCI design research
- digital wellbeing

## Research Context

The paper develops the concept of digital containers: bounded digital spaces designed to support intention, self-regulation, and reflection.

## Main Finding

Digital wellbeing tools can support intentional use by creating bounded contexts rather than merely blocking or optimizing engagement. The work emphasizes design that supports user intention.

## Limitations

This paper is not a reactance study and not a direct evaluation of DaD. It is more conceptually adjacent than decisive.

## Evidence Grade

weak to moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD can be understood as a browser-level defensive container. The point is not random restriction; it is creating bounded conditions where the user's chosen plan is easier to maintain.

## Product Implication

DaD should design protection surfaces as containers:

- show the active boundary;
- show allowed recovery paths;
- make the plan visible;
- keep the user oriented;
- avoid making the block feel like a blank wall.
