# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Rains, S. A. (2013). The Nature of Psychological Reactance Revisited: A Meta-Analytic Review. Human Communication Research, 39(1), 47-73.

Link: https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article-abstract/39/1/47/4093670

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- communication psychology

## Research Context

The paper meta-analyzes psychological reactance studies, clarifying relationships between freedom threat, anger, negative cognitions, perceived threat, and source appraisal.

## Main Finding

The meta-analysis supports the importance of perceived freedom threat in reactance processes and helps clarify reactance as an interrelated set of affective and cognitive responses.

## Limitations

The studies are mostly communication and persuasion contexts. They do not directly evaluate self-chosen software locks.

## Evidence Grade

strong for reactance mechanisms; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

The perceived threat matters. DaD can reduce perceived illegitimate threat by making enforcement feel like execution of a prior user decision rather than an external command.

## Product Implication

For strong interventions, DaD should always answer:

- What rule is active?
- Why did it trigger?
- What can the user do now?
- When can the restricted action be changed?

## Claim Boundary

What DaD may safely say:

DaD tries to reduce unnecessary freedom-threat perception by explaining active rules and preserving safe choices.

What DaD must not claim:

DaD must not claim that a user-selected lock cannot cause reactance.

## Notes

This source supports both transparency and bounded choice.
