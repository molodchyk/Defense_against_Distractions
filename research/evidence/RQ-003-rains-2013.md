# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Rains, S. A. (2013). The Nature of Psychological Reactance Revisited: A Meta-Analytic Review. Human Communication Research, 39(1), 47-73.

Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2012.01443.x

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- reactance theory

## Research Context

The paper revisited whether reactance can be modeled as cognition, affect, or an intertwined cognitive-affective state.

## Main Finding

The intertwined model best fit the meta-analytic data: reactance is best represented as a latent factor with anger and counterarguments as indicators.

## Empirical Detail

- Scope: K=20 studies, N=4,942.
- Competing path models tested different conceptualizations of reactance.
- Best-fitting model: anger plus counterarguments as intertwined indicators of reactance.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Reactance is not just "annoyance." It combines affective arousal with counterarguing, which can motivate source rejection and freedom-restoration behavior.

## Limitations

The meta-analysis concerns persuasive messages, not self-imposed browser locks.

## Evidence Grade

strong for reactance mechanism; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

If DaD creates reactance, the result may be active opposition: bypassing, disabling, source rejection, or arguing against the rule.

## Design Consequence

DaD should collect local, non-content metrics that proxy freedom-restoration pressure:

- repeated emergency escape;
- repeated queued relaxation;
- repeated Continue followed by deeper drift;
- extension disable/re-enable where available;
- prompt dismissal loops.

## What Changes

Success metrics cannot be only "blocked page count." A high block count plus rising bypass pressure may be a failing lock.

## Notes

This source grounds bypass pressure as a research-backed mechanism rather than a moral interpretation.
