# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Lyngs, U., Lukoff, K., Slovak, P., Binns, R., Slack, A., Inzlicht, M., Van Kleek, M., & Shadbolt, N. (2019). Self-Control in Cyberspace: Applying Dual Systems Theory to a Review of Digital Self-Control Tools. Proceedings of CHI 2019.

Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.00157

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3290605.3300361

## Source Type

- HCI review
- digital self-control tools

## Research Context

The paper analyzes hundreds of digital self-control tools and maps their intervention strategies.

## Main Finding

Digital self-control tools use blocking, timers, reminders, feedback, goal support, and friction. The design space is broad, and tools vary in how they support reflection, restraint, and behavior change.

## Limitations

This is a design-space review, not proof that every intervention is effective. It does not specifically test autonomy-supportive enforcement.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD combines multiple digital self-control mechanisms. This supports using a ladder of interventions instead of a single block action.

## Product Implication

DaD should keep proportionality:

- diagnostics before force where possible;
- reversible page-local interventions before hard blocks;
- hard enforcement mainly when precommitted or clearly high-risk;
- reflection and recovery actions rather than only denial.

## Notes

This source supports the intervention ladder and proportionality.
