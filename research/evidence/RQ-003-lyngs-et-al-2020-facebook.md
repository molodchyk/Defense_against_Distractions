# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Lyngs, U., Lukoff, K., Slovak, P., Seymour, W., Webb, H., Jirotka, M., Zhao, J., Van Kleek, M., & Shadbolt, N. (2020). "I Just Want to Hack Myself to Not Get Distracted": Evaluating Design Interventions for Self-Control on Facebook. CHI 2020.

Link: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.04180

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376672

## Source Type

- primary HCI study
- browser-based self-control intervention

## Research Context

The study compared goal reminders and newsfeed removal for Facebook self-control. It measured use patterns, perceived control, and post-intervention effects.

## Main Finding

Both interventions helped participants stay on task, but they also changed device routing. Many participants used smartphone access to manage the intervention's downsides while preserving some laptop protection.

## Empirical Detail

- Goal reminders reduced daily visits from a baseline median of 29.4 to 10.6 during intervention.
- Goal reminders reduced average daily scrolling by 42%.
- No-feed reduced average daily scrolling by 73%.
- Cross-device access changed for 86% of participants in the goal-reminder condition and 57% in the no-feed condition, compared with 6% in control.
- Qualitative reports described phone use as sometimes more deliberate and context-separated.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Bypass-like behavior can be an adaptive workaround. Users may preserve protection in one context while moving legitimate or less dangerous use to another context with higher access cost.

## Limitations

This was Facebook-specific and does not prove cross-device bypass is good. DaD cannot infer phone behavior unless explicitly designed for it.

## Evidence Grade

strong for browser self-control design; moderate for DaD bypass interpretation.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should not automatically treat workaround behavior as a defect or moral failure. The pattern matters.

## Design Consequence

Bypass diagnostics should separate:

- same-device immediate re-entry into drift;
- repeated emergency escape;
- repeated isolate into the same feed;
- deliberate context separation;
- legitimate task-specific exceptions.

## What Changes

DaD's local diagnostics should classify bypass pressure as ambiguous evidence requiring review or threshold adjustment, not immediate punitive escalation.

## Notes

This evidence also supports DaD's Isolate action as a way to legalize legitimate branches rather than forcing all deviation into covert workarounds.
