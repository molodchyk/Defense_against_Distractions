# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Roffarello, A. M., & De Russis, L. (2023). Achieving Digital Wellbeing Through Digital Self-Control Tools: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 30(4), 1-66.

Link: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3571810

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3571810

## Source Type

- systematic review
- meta-analysis
- HCI

## Research Context

The review synthesizes research on digital self-control tools, their strategies, and evaluation patterns.

## Main Finding

Digital self-control tools show promise, but the evidence base has gaps and needs better long-term, ethical, and context-sensitive evaluation.

## Limitations

The source does not resolve DaD's exact hard-block policy or prompt design.

## Evidence Grade

strong for the state of digital self-control evidence; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should be research-informed but humble. Strong interventions need local validation and careful evaluation because the broader field still lacks definitive evidence for many tool designs.

## Product Implication

DaD should locally track non-sensitive outcomes:

- intervention frequency;
- return/isolate/continue choices;
- blocked relaxation attempts;
- emergency escape use;
- post-lock adjustment;
- false-positive feedback.
