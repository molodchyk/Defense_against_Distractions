# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Roffarello, A. M., & De Russis, L. (2023). Achieving Digital Wellbeing Through Digital Self-Control Tools: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis. ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction, 30(4), Article 53.

Link: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3571810

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1145/3571810

## Source Type

- systematic review
- meta-analysis
- HCI / digital self-control tools

## Research Context

The paper reviewed current work on digital self-control tools, including motivations, strategies, design choices, effectiveness, and evaluation challenges.

## Main Finding

Digital self-control tools show short-term promise, but the field has limited long-term evidence and needs better context-sensitive, ethical, and standardized evaluation.

## Empirical Detail

- Scope: systematic review and meta-analysis of digital self-control tools.
- Tools include timers, lockouts, self-monitoring, and other interventions.
- The authors estimate an overall effect on reducing unwanted technology use.
- Their field-level conclusions emphasize short-term effects, limited long-term habit evidence, overfocus on screen time, and the need for better evaluation.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Reduced usage time is not the same as improved agency or wellbeing. A tool can reduce time while creating frustration, attrition, or context mismatch.

## Limitations

The public abstract and available summaries do not provide enough detail to map exact effect sizes to DaD intervention thresholds.

## Evidence Grade

strong for state of field; moderate for specific DaD decisions.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should validate locally instead of assuming its intervention ladder works for every nervous system state or user context.

## Design Consequence

Use local privacy-preserving outcome metrics:

- Return after prompt;
- Continue followed by deeper drift;
- queued relaxation;
- emergency escape;
- disable/uninstall signals where possible;
- post-lock review behavior.

## What Changes

DaD's research pipeline should prioritize local validation of intervention fit, not just scientific justification for adding more blocks.
