# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: John, A. (2015). When Commitment Fails: Evidence from a Regular Saver Product in the Philippines. STICERD Economic Organisation and Public Policy Discussion Paper.

Link: https://sticerd.lse.ac.uk/dps/eopp/eopp55.pdf

## Source Type

- primary study
- randomized field experiment
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

The study examines a regular-installment commitment savings product. Participants chose a savings plan and penalty. The paper studies when commitment can fail because people choose contracts that do not match their actual future behavior.

## Main Finding

Commitment can backfire when people choose poorly calibrated commitment contracts. The study found substantial default, suggesting that many users selected contracts that were too weak or otherwise ill-suited. The paper argues that partial self-knowledge can lead people into harmful commitment: enough awareness to want a device, but not enough to choose the right one.

## Limitations

This is a financial product in a low-income setting, not a browser extension. Penalties were monetary and repeated; DaD's penalties are access restrictions and friction. Still, the design warning is directly relevant.

## Evidence Grade

moderate.

## Relevance To DaD

This is the main caution source for locked schedules. The existence of commitment-device evidence does not mean DaD should encourage unlimited harsh locks. Users can overestimate their future compliance or set locks that are too broad, too weak, too long, or too hard to recover from.

## Product Implication

DaD should avoid turning locked schedules into a trap. Recommended safeguards:

- show lock consequences before activation;
- allow stricter edits but not silent scope expansion with unclear consequences;
- prefer delayed relaxation over permanent impossibility where feasible;
- preserve emergency access semantics;
- provide post-lock review: "Did this lock help or harm?";
- keep defaults conservative.

## Notes

This source should keep DaD honest. Defense is not the same as maximum force.
