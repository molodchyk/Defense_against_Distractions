# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: John, A. (2014). When Commitment Fails: Evidence from a Regular Saver Product in the Philippines. STICERD Economic Organisation and Public Policy Discussion Paper 055.

Link: https://ideas.repec.org/p/cep/stieop/055.html

PDF: https://sticerd.lse.ac.uk/dps/eopp/eopp55.pdf

## Source Type

- primary study
- randomized field experiment
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

Low-income individuals in the Philippines were offered a regular-installment savings commitment. Participants chose the stakes of their own default penalty.

## Main Finding

Commitment can fail even when users choose it voluntarily. The study found large savings effects, but a majority of commitment adopters defaulted and paid their self-chosen penalties.

## Empirical Detail

- Context: regular-installment savings product with self-chosen default penalty.
- Outcome: 55 percent of clients defaulted on the commitment contract.
- Interpretation in the paper: many participants chose weak or ill-suited contracts; take-up and default were negatively predicted by sophisticated hyperbolic discounting.
- Product risk: partial self-knowledge can be worse than none: enough to want commitment, not enough to choose the right commitment.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The dangerous zone is not only over-strict commitment. A commitment can also be too weak to change behavior yet still strong enough to impose costs. A user can be harmed by a self-chosen contract because the contract fails at its own job.

## Limitations

This is a financial product in a low-income context. DaD's costs are access friction, frustration, and bypass pressure rather than money. The study does not directly estimate attention outcomes.

## Evidence Grade

strong for commitment miscalibration; moderate for direct DaD policy.

## Relevance To DaD

This is the central warning for locked schedules. Repeated lock conflict should not be interpreted as "the user is weak." It may mean the lock is the wrong size, time, scope, or escape design.

## Design Consequence

DaD should record local lock outcomes and support post-lock calibration. Repeated failed locks should lead to review, not automatic escalation.

## What Changes

This changes the locked-schedule model from enforcement-only to calibration-aware enforcement. The product should distinguish useful resistance from contract failure.

## Notes

This source is especially relevant to emergency escape, queued relaxation, and post-lock review.
