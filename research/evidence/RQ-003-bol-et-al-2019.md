# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Bol, N., Smit, E. S., Lustria, M. L. A., et al. (2019). Identifying the Most Autonomy-Supportive Message Frame in Digital Health Communication: A 2x2 Between-Subjects Experiment. Journal of Medical Internet Research, 21(10), e14074.

Link: https://www.jmir.org/2019/10/e14074/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.2196/14074

## Source Type

- primary study
- digital communication

## Research Context

The study tested autonomy-supportive language and choice provision in a digital health communication context.

## Main Finding

Providing choice can be a practical way to improve digital communication, especially for users with a higher need for autonomy.

## Limitations

This is messaging research, not enforcement research. It does not mean DaD should offer unrestricted choice during a locked schedule.

## Evidence Grade

weak to moderate for DaD.

## Relevance To DaD

Even when DaD cannot offer the forbidden option, it can offer safe choices:

- return;
- inspect graph;
- isolate if allowed;
- add protection;
- queue relaxation;
- wait until lock ends;
- use emergency escape if configured.

## Product Implication

DaD should avoid dead-end screens. A blocked relaxation attempt should still leave the user with meaningful next actions.

## Notes

This supports choice within protection, not choice to bypass every protection.
