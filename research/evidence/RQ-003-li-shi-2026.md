# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Li, J., & Shi, Z. (2026). How Antifreedom Messages Shape Perceptions and Persuasion: A Meta-Analysis of Psychological Reactance Theory. Human Communication Research, 52(1), 38-55.

Link: https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article/52/1/38/8178818

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/hcr/hqaf016

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- communication psychology

## Research Context

The paper meta-analyzes how antifreedom messages affect persuasion-related outcomes, including effects on perceived freedom threat, reactance, and message outcomes.

## Main Finding

The meta-analysis supports the idea that antifreedom messages are reliably associated with reactance-related responses and persuasion outcomes. Perceived freedom threat is central.

## Limitations

The source is about messages, not direct software lockouts. It should guide wording and explanation, not determine enforcement thresholds.

## Evidence Grade

strong for communication effects; moderate for DaD application.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should avoid antifreedom phrasing even when it enforces a lock. "You cannot" and "DaD forbids" are weaker than "Your locked schedule is active" and "This change would relax the active plan."

## Product Implication

DaD should define a style rule for enforcement copy:

- describe the active rule;
- describe the effect of the attempted action;
- describe available alternatives;
- avoid commanding, scolding, or identity labels.

## Notes

This is a current meta-analysis and should guide localization copy too.
