# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Li, Z., & Shi, J. (2026). Message Effects on Psychological Reactance: Meta-Analyses. Human Communication Research, 52(1), 38-52.

Link: https://academic.oup.com/hcr/article/52/1/38/8178818

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1093/hcr/hqaf016

## Source Type

- meta-analysis
- communication psychology
- reactance research

## Research Context

The paper meta-analyzed studies from 2005 to 2024 that experimentally manipulated message features and measured anger, negative cognitions, and psychological reactance.

## Main Finding

High freedom-threatening language increased reactance-related outcomes; gain versus loss framing did not produce meaningful differences.

## Empirical Detail

- Scope: 28 articles, 33 studies, 146 effect sizes.
- High freedom-threatening language increased anger (k=28, r=.21).
- High freedom-threatening language increased negative cognitions (k=25, r=.17).
- High freedom-threatening language increased psychological reactance (k=53, r=.20).
- Gain versus loss framing showed no significant differences in anger (k=6, r=-.03), negative cognitions (k=6, r=-.01), or reactance (k=12, r=-.02).
- Reactance proxies were negatively associated with persuasion outcomes: anger (r=-.23, k=42), negative cognitions (r=-.18, k=39).

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

The research distinguishes two things that are often blurred: generic negative framing and explicit freedom threat. The latter is the reactance risk.

## Limitations

This is persuasive-message research, not browser-lock research. It does not define lock thresholds.

## Evidence Grade

strong for message-feature effects; moderate for DaD UI copy.

## Relevance To DaD

DaD should avoid controlling, imperative, persuasion-like language. But this is not because "negative copy is always bad"; it is because freedom-threatening language has measured reactance effects.

## Design Consequence

Enforcement copy should name:

- active rule;
- current restriction;
- available actions;
- unlock/review path.

It should not try to convince, shame, or command.

## What Changes

Copy review should focus on freedom-threat language, not just whether a message sounds positive or negative.

## Notes

This source lets DaD be strict while avoiding needless linguistic reactance.
