# Evidence Card

## Source

Citation: Gine, X., Karlan, D., & Zinman, J. (2010). Put Your Money Where Your Butt Is: A Commitment Contract for Smoking Cessation. American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 2(4), 213-235.

Link: https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.213

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1257/app.2.4.213

## Source Type

- primary study
- randomized field experiment
- health behavior
- behavioral economics

## Research Context

Smokers in the Philippines were offered CARES, a voluntary commitment savings account. They deposited money for six months and recovered it only if they passed urine tests for nicotine and cotinine.

## Main Finding

Take-up was low, but assignment to the commitment offer produced a lasting increase in smoking cessation outcomes.

## Empirical Detail

- Product: voluntary savings account with forfeiture if the smoker failed the test.
- Take-up: 11 percent of smokers offered CARES accepted.
- Six-month outcome: those randomly offered CARES were 3 percentage points more likely to pass the urine test than control.
- Persistence: the effect persisted in surprise tests at 12 months.

## Non-Obvious Mechanism

Population-level effects can look modest because only a subset wants or can use hard commitment. Low adoption does not mean the device is weak for the subgroup that needs it.

## Limitations

Smoking cessation has addiction and health stakes unlike browsing. The monetary forfeiture mechanism is stronger than DaD's access restrictions.

## Evidence Grade

strong for voluntary hard commitment in health behavior; moderate for DaD.

## Relevance To DaD

Strong locks may be crucial for some users even if they should not be universal defaults. DaD should preserve the hard-lock layer while keeping it opt-in and scoped.

## Design Consequence

Do not remove hard locks because they are intense. Instead, keep them explicit, local, recoverable through emergency design, and surrounded by calibration review.

## What Changes

This prevents overcorrecting toward only soft interventions. DaD needs a defense ladder, including hard commitment for users and moments where soft friction is not enough.

## Notes

This is a counterweight to the "hard commitments can fail" evidence.
